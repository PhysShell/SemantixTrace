## 3. The AS ≡ AIS identity is now a reproduced fact

Our `--level service` and `--level inner` runs produce **identical rank
vectors** per system, matching the identical committed author logs and the
identical Nezha rows of paper Tables 3/4. Combined with the source fact
that both evaluation functions execute the same matching logic
(01-nezha-artifact-audit.md, claim M), the artifact demonstrably never
computes a distinct service-level metric. What is reported as AS@k *is*
the inner-service computation relabeled.

## 4. Run-to-run stability

Second identical runs (`*-run2`) from pristine drain3 state:

- Aggregate metrics and rank vectors: **identical** in both systems.
- Post-run drain3 template state: **byte-identical** (SHA256) in both.
- Per-case candidate lists: hipster **56/56 byte-identical**; ts **38/45**
  identical, 4 reordered (equal-score ties), **3 with different candidate
  composition** (alarm-dedup keeps "the deepest" resource candidate, and
  depth/pod attribution breaks ties by `ProcessPoolExecutor` completion
  order — `pattern_ranker.py:161-186`, `get_event_depth_pod`).

Consequence: the metrics are stable, but the *explanation* shown to an
operator (which alternative candidates appear, in which order) is not
deterministic on TT. Any interpretability claim built on list composition
below the matched candidate is unstable.

## 5. Candidate-set sizes (why the benchmark is friendlier than it looks)

- OB: min 1, **median 2**, max 28 candidates per case.
- TT: min 4, **median 18**, max 63.

With a median of 2 candidates on OB, top-1/3/5 distinctions barely exist;
the pipeline's alarm stage (fixed thresholds, first-alarm-only) plus
root-most pruning already collapse the search space before "ranking" does
any work. This bounds how much *any* better ranker can show on OB.

## 6. Failure during reproduction (recorded, not hidden)

The first concurrent execution OOM-killed both OB configs
(memory-cgroup kills of ~670 MB workers; artifacts preserved under
`results/e0/hipster-*-FAILED-oom/`). Resolution: D-004 — env-configurable
worker count (fork commit `ae34750`, default 64 unchanged), sequential
re-run at 8 workers. Validation gate (rank-vector equality with author
logs) passed.

## 7. Computational cost

- OB: ~40 s per fault case (2226 s / 56 cases, 8 workers, 4 CPUs).
- TT: ~8 s per fault case (360 s / 45).
- The paper's efficiency claim (16 s / 30 s per 50k-event window on a
  256 GB Xeon) is *order-of-magnitude consistent* with ours but was not
  independently verified (different hardware, different worker counts).

## 8. What E0 does NOT establish

- The artifact's *internal* silent data loss (whole-trace drops under
  `except Exception: pass`) is not quantified — the artifact does not
  expose it and E0 runs it unmodified. An observation-only shim is listed
  for E2's importer parity checks, where the same telemetry must be counted
  on both sides.
- Nothing about the *quality* of the evaluation semantics — that is E1
  (`03-e1-measurement-audit.md`).

## 9. E0 verdict on the milestone question

**"Which of Nezha's 92.86% / 86.67% are real and why":** both numbers are
**real as artifact outputs**: bit-exact reproducible, arithmetically
faithful per the independent evaluator, and stable across repeated runs.
What is *not* real is their labeling: they are not service-level AS@1
values distinct from inner-service AIS@1 — the artifact computes one
metric and prints it under both names — and the pipeline that produces
them is handed the fault time (`inject_time + 2 min`) because the paper's
Anomaly Detector does not exist in the artifact. How much the numbers'
*meaning* survives corrected semantics is quantified in E1.
